# Health Information and Children

Read when the product concerns health, other sensitive information, younger users, or age-dependent features.

Determine the actual audience, data, purposes, actors, geography, and whether sector-specific rules apply. Health-related software is not automatically subject to every health law, and being a general consumer app does not remove data-protection obligations.

For children, distinguish contract capacity, privacy consent, parental authorization, safety rules, and vendor age limits. Applicable ages vary by jurisdiction and service; do not hardcode one global threshold.

Review minimization, role-based access, sharing defaults, deletion, retention, age handling, and any AI processing. Avoid collecting additional identity documents merely to add an age check without a justified design.

Escalate specific high-impact legal or clinical uncertainty to qualified review. Do not invent regulatory approvals or claim a privacy notice solves a sensitive processing issue.

Start from applicable primary sources, including [GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng), [IMY](https://www.imy.se/), [US children's privacy guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/privacy-security/childrens-privacy), or [HHS HIPAA guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/) when the scope calls for them.
